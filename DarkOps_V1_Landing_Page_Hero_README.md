# DarkOps V1 Landing Page — Hero Section

## Purpose

The DarkOps V1 landing page should communicate four things clearly:

- What DarkOps is.
- What DarkOps actually does.
- What makes its approach different.
- What a customer can expect before trusting or adopting it.

The page should prioritize **focus, credibility, transparency, and product explanation** rather than fabricated social proof, customer marquees, testimonials, or inflated claims.

# 2. The Core Philosophy

## Heading

**Aim closer. Your application's security needs more focus.**

## Main Copy

DarkOps is an application-centric ITDM&R platform built to monitor security activity, investigate what matters, and help neutralize insider risks before they impact your systems.

It tracks the security state of connected applications, assets, events, alerts, incidents, and internal access without turning security into a wall of manual dashboards.

> **Connect it. Let it work. Come back when it matters.**

## Principles

### High-Risk Alerts

Stay informed and respond with control when something actually matters.

### Accurate Solutions

Investigate security issues with grounded findings, clear reasoning, and actions that remain on your terms.

### Grounded System Processes

No unnecessary hardware burden, no AI theater, and no promises beyond what DarkOps can actually observe and do.

# Design Direction

## Overall

DarkOps V1 should feel:

- Focused
- Technical
- Transparent
- Credible
- Calm under pressure
- Distinct from generic cybersecurity branding

## Visual Strategy

Avoid filling empty space merely to make the page look larger.

Use:

- Large Foldit statements for major ideas.
- Montserrat for UI and supporting copy.
- Dark cinematic sections where appropriate.
- Subtle geometric/perspective decorative patterns behind content.
- The DarkOps agent/scribble identity artwork selectively.
- The target as the custom `O` in the wordmark and as a secondary detection symbol.
- Real product/dashboard visuals whenever possible.

The decorative background pattern is **environmental decoration only**. It is not a customer logo marquee and must not imply that companies use or endorse DarkOps.

---

# Product Story

The landing page should explain the DarkOps model progressively:

```text
Application
    ↓
Detection
    ↓
Event
    ↓
Alert
    ↓
Incident
    ↓
Vulnerability
    ↓
Response
    ↓
```

This lifecycle is more valuable for V1 credibility than pretending DarkOps already has a large customer base, testimonials, awards, or enterprise endorsements.

# Frontend Convention

`index.css` is reserved for genuinely global application styling.

Page/component-specific styling should remain with the JSX component itself, primarily through Tailwind classes and local style objects where appropriate.

Example:

```text
core/pages/landing/
├── LandingPage.jsx
├── HeroSection.jsx
└── DashboardPreview.jsx
```

Do not turn `index.css` into a dumping ground for every landing-page section.
